Temperature → load

How to use it for your own read, hour by hour: compare tomorrow's forecast with the actual two days earlier, same hour. Heating: every °C colder below 15 °C adds the heat number (warmer takes it away). Cooling: every °C hotter feels-like above 20 °C adds the cool number (cooler takes it away). Between 15 and 20 °C load barely moves: 0. Add the two → MW. +250 MW or more = +1 on the card (load jumping vs two days ago), −250 or less = −1. If you cross 15 °C (e.g. 18 → 10), only the part below 15 counts (5 °C).

Morning routine

  1. Score each hour by eye (Card column). Load gap (Meteologica load − IESO, Tesla when Meteologica is missing; using whichever forecast has the star tested worse): +250 or more = +2, +100 to +250 = +1, same on the minus side. Wind gap (IESO wind − Meteologica): +200 = +1, −200 = −1. If public spare is under 2,500, double the load and wind points. Wind ramp into the hour: −300 or more = +1 (wind leaving), +300 = −1 (wind arriving). Wind upside: highest of the 9 weather models 800+ MW above IESO wind = −1 (more wind could still show up). DA vs usual: +18 = −2, +8 = −1, −8 = +1 (no +2 on the cheap side). NY − Ontario (NY converted to CAD): +$10 = +1, −$10 = −1. Exports (Exports line in the Market changes box / Exp column): model has net exports 500+ MW below the usual 14-day level = −1 (surplus stays home), 500+ MW above = +1. Temp swing: tomorrow's temperature vs the actual two days earlier, turned into MW with the Temperature → load table below the hour-by-hour table (heating below 15 °C, cooling on feels-like above 20 °C, by season and time of day): +250 MW or more = +1 (load jump), −250 or less = −1. It earns its money on the edge of a tier, not on its own.
  2. Soft system check: public spare over 5,500 and wind over 2,000 → any long gets an amber flag. Longs there had RT above DA in about 1 in 3 hours (other longs about 47%), median about −$6, with rare spikes paying for them. It is a flag, not a rule, until the live record says otherwise.
  3. Check the physics read (Read column) and what drives it (bar). It is the same physics with weights relearned on the last 90 days.
  4. Tier A: card ±3 or more and the read on the same side → full size. Tier C: card ±3 but the read disagrees → stand aside. Tier B: card under ±3 and read ±5 or more → follow the read. Neutral: no tested edge.
  5. Season check (strong-card hours, Jun 2025 – Oct 2026): shorts won in fall both years (about +$9/MWh in 2025 and 2026), in summer both years (+$2 in 2025, +$11–14 in 2026) and in winter 25/26 (+$5–6); they lost in spring 2026 (−$1.5 to −$2.4). Spring before 2026 can't be tested: the DA market only started May 2025. Longs won in every season except fall 2026 (−$3.5). Most hours settle RT under DA in every month, so the season sets which side the spikes favour, not the usual hour.

Reading order

  1. Is DA rich? CAHR 10+, headroom under 7,000, DA fc 10+ over usual. Rich = a short has something to collect.
  2. What could go wrong that DA can't see? In order of weight: (1) Load vs IESO: Tesla and Dynasty above IESO; if both are 300+ under, discount the bullish signs below (it does not by itself make a short). (2) Wind falling into the hour (IESO 3 h change), worst when the forecast is windy. (3) Gas need ramping (3 h, 500+). (4) Wind uncertainty: wide models low–high gap, use for size. (5) Net-load 3 h change, mostly the same as the gas ramp. Little weight: wind level, solar change, IESO wind vs models low. Check the hour before too.
  3. When? Sunset hour and the next; morning ramp; off-ramp HE20–21; headroom 7,000–8,500.
  4. How sure? Wind models' spread, load vendors far apart, weekends and holidays, missing data.
  5. Size for the worst hour, not the average.

    Market read: where is the market likely wrong?

    The market prices each hour off the public picture: IESO's load and wind, so IESO's gas spare. Our private picture uses the forecasts that have been most accurate (Meteologica load and wind, else the vendor and weather-model consensus). When the market sees an hour as tight but our picture is looser (something will save it), DA tends to clear rich and RT collapses: that is the short. When the market sees it as calm but our picture is tighter (something can go wrong), DA is cheap and the spike is unpriced: that is the long. When both pictures are tight, or the wind models disagree, the answer depends on how much fear DA has already priced: our DA forecast well above the usual level for the hour means it is over-paid (short); near usual means the danger is unpriced (stand aside or small long). Rules and statistics use 2026 only; they were learned on Jan–Sep 2026 and checked blind on October. Display only: nothing here changes the ladders.

    How each read has settled

    Market scared + danger: is the danger already in the price?

    2026 only. When the market is scared and we also see danger, the question is whether DA has already paid for it. The best tell known at the bid is our DA forecast versus the usual level for that hour: well above usual, the danger was over-paid and shorts won; near usual, it was not priced and shorts lost. The crowd's position (IESO posts each day's virtual trades at 12:33 EST the day before delivery, so at the bid we see the positions for today, not tomorrow) points the same way but more weakly.

    Is it truly tight? Priced scarcity vs the physics

    Fair RT is what the physical picture alone says RT should be: gas spare (public and ours), gas expected online, the gas ramp into the hour, the wind level and its 3-hour change, the net-load ramp, how far apart the wind models are, load and wind versus IESO, valves, outages and the hour. No prices go into it. Gap = our DA forecast − fair RT: positive means the market is paying for scarcity the physics doesn't support; negative means the physics is tighter than the price. Calls use the gap against its own last 6 weeks. Gas need and spare here are on the backtest basis (14-day median of actual flows), so they differ a little from the Fundamentals tab.

    How each hour's RT − DA moves with the other hours

    How to read it. Each cell asks: on days when HE x came in further above (or below) DA than usual, did HE y do the same? 100% = always together, 0% = unrelated, negative (red) = opposite. It is a rank correlation, so one spike day can't create a link. A cell is shown only when the link is both at least moderate (40%+) and statistically real at the 1% level for the number of days picked; blank = too weak to count. Hover a cell for the exact value and roughly how much of the moves it explains. Why it matters: if you are long HE17 and HE18 and they move together 65%, that is closer to one bet at double size than two trades, so size them together. The window that best predicts the next two weeks is all history; 7–14 days is noisy, use it only to spot a change.
