What each column means
Signal / why
tight: spare gas + hydro below the learned headroom threshold (7,000 MW so far), so DA commits peakers and Lennox that RT often does not need. surplus: the bid-time gas need (next column) is below the learned threshold, so Ontario is long on nuclear, hydro and wind and RT tends to settle under DA. ext means the looser surplus threshold, traded at half size.
Spare MW (headroom)
Gas + hydro available minus what IESO's pre-deadline Adequacy forecast says they must cover. It is the Ontario version of the Alberta cushion. Below ~7,000 MW, prices spike and Lennox runs.
Gas need
IESO demand forecast − nuclear available − wind forecast − solar forecast − expected hydro + expected net exports (hydro and exports are the last 14 days' median DA schedule for that hour). It tracks the DA gas schedule (corr 0.82, about 1,000 MW error). Under ~3,000 MW, sells won about 72–95% of hours.
Lennox
How often Lennox (the oil/gas steam plant, the most expensive unit) actually ran in RT at this level of spare MW this summer: 82% below 6,000 MW, 62% at 6–7k, 35% at 7–8k, 13% at 8–9k, 2% above 9k. Hours when Lennox ran averaged DA − RT of +$22; hours without it averaged +$0.3.
Lean
A small regression's estimate of DA − RT in $/MWh from the bid-time inputs. The ranking holds up (the top fifth of hours averaged +$5.70, the bottom fifth −$2.99), but it is too weak to trade on alone. Information only.
Tesla − IESO
Tesla's load forecast minus IESO's pre-deadline demand forecast, in MW. IESO runs high: actual load came in below IESO's forecast in 73% of hours, by 377 MW on average, while Tesla's bias was only +26 MW. When Tesla is 600+ MW below IESO, DA − RT averaged +$13 (68% sell wins), but walk-forward that was only +$1.86 and not reliable, so it is not a trigger.
ON→NY sched
NYISO's day-ahead scheduled flow from Ontario into New York (MW). It is posted about 09:40 ET, before IESO closes, and matches IESO's actual NY flow closely (corr 0.81, same direction 91% of the time), so it is an early read of the NY intertie. It adds nothing to the price forecast beyond the NY price itself. Information only.
RT − DA if load 200–600 low / high
Sensitivity, not a forecast: the median RT − DA this summer in hours with similar spare MW when actual load came in 200–600 MW below (left) or above (right) IESO's forecast. Overall: load 600+ MW low → RT about $15 under DA; 200–600 low → $5 under; 200–600 high → $5 over; 600+ high → $13 over.

How to read Ontario's mix (vs Alberta)

  • Nuclear (~9 GW) runs flat around the clock and never sets price on the way up. What matters is how much is out: every MW of outage is a MW gas must cover.
  • Gas is the price-setter. The DA gas schedule explains price better than anything (corr 0.64). Under 4 GW of gas, the system is in surplus and DA overshoots RT. Between 4 and 7 GW, efficient gas plants set price in both markets and there is no edge. Above 7 GW, DA commits peakers and Lennox and again overshoots. It is U-shaped, unlike Alberta's one-sided cushion.
  • Hydro (~3–4 GW scheduled) is the flexible buffer. It moves with gas, so treat it as a response, not a cause.
  • Wind (up to ~4.9 GW) is the main supply surprise. 1,000 MW more wind in RT than DA scheduled moved DA − RT by about +$22.
  • Demand miss is the biggest single driver of DA − RT (corr −0.45). 1,000 MW less load than IESO forecast moved DA − RT by about +$18.
  • Exports: 1,000 MW more export in RT than DA scheduled moved DA − RT by about −$19.
  • Solar (478 MW on the grid) barely matters.

Day by day record
DA columns are daily means (model vs published, and the error). The spread and P&L columns use RT: "sell all" sells 1 MW every hour, "signal P&L" sells only the signal hours. Hours are in IESO time. Every hour is also saved in data/es_da_errors_hourly_*.csv and data/bt_signals_v2_*.csv.

What is in the forecast

  • DA: IESO's pre-deadline Adequacy forecast (spare gas + hydro after demand, nuclear, wind, solar), NYISO Zone A day-ahead price and NYISO's scheduled NY–Ontario flow (both posted 09:33–09:55 ET), yesterday's DA, and the Ontario Hub forward with Dawn gas and PJM West for the daily level.
  • RT: the DA forecast plus the spread distribution for that time block and tightness. The range is reliable; the single RT number is not (hourly RT noise is about $18).
  • Signals (v2): SELL when spare MW is under the learned threshold (tight) or the bid-time gas need is under its learned threshold (surplus). SELL-L uses the looser surplus threshold at half size. Both thresholds are re-learned each morning from spreads settled by then. There is no buy rule: none made money in the walk-forward test. The old v1 rules (8,500 MW in HE14–21, Tesla ±600) were retired because walk-forward they earned +$3.45/MWh with a 90% range that went below zero.
  • Lennox: how often Lennox ran in RT at each level of spare MW this summer (82% under 6,000 MW, 2% above 9,000). Lennox hours averaged DA − RT of +$22.
  • Not modelled: forced outages after the deadline, virtual uplift, and winter hourly shapes (hourly prices only exist since 28 Jun 2026).
